\section{Introduction} \label{intro}
In this short paper we will show the quadratic formula.
In Section \ref{sec2} we will define some preliminaries related to quadratics and roots.
In Section \ref{sec3} we will provide a proof of the quadratic formula.
Finally, in Section \ref{sec4} we will see some examples of the quadratic formula in action.


\section{Preliminaries} \label{sec2}
First, we define a quadratic function.

\begin{defn}
    A quadratic is a polynomial of degree 2. A quadratic has the following form:
    \[ f(x) = a x^2 + bx + c, \]
    where \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(a \ne 0\).
\end{defn}

Now we define a root of a quadratic.
\begin{defn}
    Let \(x \in \mathbb{R}\). Then \(x\) is a root of the quadratic \(f\) if \(f(x) = 0\).
\end{defn}


\section{Proof} \label{sec3}
We are finally ready to show our main result.

\begin{thm}
    Let \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(a \ne 0\).
    Let \(f\) be the quadratic given by \(f(x) = a x^2 + bx + c\).
    Then \(x\) is a root of \(f\) if and only if \[ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} \]
    and \(x\) given by the equation is real.

    \begin{proof}
        Let \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(a \ne 0\).
        Let \(f\) be the quadratic given by \[f(x) = a x^2 + bx + c.\]

        First we check that if \(x=\frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\) and \(x\) is real,
        then \(x\) is a root of \(f\).

        Let \(x = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\).
        Then we have the following:
        \begin{align*}
        f(x) &= a \left(\frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\right)^2
                + b \left(\frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\right) + c \\
             &= a \frac{\left(-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}\right)^2}{\left(2a\right)^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 + b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= a \frac{b^2 - 2b \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} + b^2 - 4 a c}{4a^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 + b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 - 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 + b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 - 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2}
             + \frac{-2ab^2 + 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{4a^2} + \frac{4a^2c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 - 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c
                -2ab^2 + 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} + 4a^2c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 -2ab^2 + 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}
                + 4a^2c  - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{0}{4a^2} \\
             &= 0. \\
        \end{align*}

        Now we consider the other case. Let \(x = \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\).
        Then we have the following:

        \begin{align*}
        f(x) &= a \left(\frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\right)^2
                + b \left(\frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\right) + c \\
             &= a \frac{\left(-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}\right)^2}{\left(2a\right)^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 - b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= a \frac{b^2 + 2b \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} + b^2 - 4 a c}{4a^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 - b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 + 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2}
                + \frac{-b^2 - b\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a} + c \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 + 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2}
             + \frac{-2ab^2 - 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{4a^2} + \frac{4a^2c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 + 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 4 a^2 c
                -2ab^2 - 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} + 4a^2c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{2 a b^2 -2ab^2 + 2ab\sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c} - 2ab \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}
                + 4a^2c  - 4 a^2 c}{4a^2} \\
             &= \frac{0}{4a^2} \\
             &= 0. \\
        \end{align*}

        So if \(x = \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\) or
        \(x = \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}\) and \(x\) is real,
        then \(f(x) = 0\), so \(x\) is a root of \(f\).

        Now we check the other direction, that the only roots of \(f\) are of the form
        \[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4 a c}}{2a}.\]

        We want to solve the equation \(f(x) = 0\). By completing the square, we get
        the following:
        \begin{align*}
          0 &= f(x) \\
            &= ax^2 + bx + c \\
            &= x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{c}{a} \\
            &= x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2x + \frac{c}{a} -
            \left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 \\
            &= \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 + \frac{c}{a} - \frac{b^2}{4a^2} \\
          \frac{b^2}{4a^2} - \frac{c}{a} &= \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 \\
          \frac{b^2 - 4a c}{4a^2} &= \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 \\
        \end{align*}

        Now we want to take the square root of the LHS so that we can continue to
        solve for \(x\). However, we now need to consider 3 cases.

        Case 1: \(\frac{b^2 - 4a c}{4a^2} > 0\). Then we are free to take the square root of
        both sides.

        \begin{align*}
          \frac{b^2 - 4a c}{4a^2} &= \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 \\
          \pm \sqrt{\frac{b^2 - 4a c}{4a^2}} &= x + \frac{b}{2a} \\
          \frac{\pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4a c}}{2a} &= x + \frac{b}{2a} \\
          \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4a c}}{2a} &= x\\
        \end{align*}

        So we have 2 real solutions, \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4a c}}{2a}\).

        Case 2: \(\frac{b^2 - 4a c}{4a^2} = 0\).

        \begin{align*}
          0 &= \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 \\
        \end{align*}

    \end{proof}
\end{thm}
